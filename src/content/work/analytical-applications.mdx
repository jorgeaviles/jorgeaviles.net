## What I developed

I built the Audit Portal with ASP.NET Core and Blazor Interactive Server to connect analytical investigation with operational audit work. The application brings record search, audit detail, lifecycle and progress views, documents, change history, and workbook exports into a permission-controlled workspace.

The project grew from a gap between legacy reporting and the investigations we wanted to pursue. Consultation with Suffolk University on machine-learning proposals in Jupyter notebooks informed the early exploration. The analytical data platform supplied a reusable foundation; the portal connected that foundation with the team's workflows.

## My role and collaboration

I am the sole hands-on developer across the analytical notebooks, data platform, and Audit Portal. Audit & Oversight colleagues contribute firsthand knowledge of attorney billing and casework. Their feedback informs analytical assumptions, model iteration, reports, and interface design.

An IT applications-team database analyst worked with me on scheduled master-data extracts. Infrastructure colleagues assisted with role-based access and application-identity permissions. These contributions and the university consultation are distinct from my coding responsibilities.

## From requirements to working capabilities

Search and filters lead from record discovery to a detailed audit workspace. The interface distinguishes stable context, subsequent progress, milestones, and recorded changes. Permitted users edit and save deliberately, with handling for unsaved work and conflicting changes. Validation accommodates specified gaps in historical records rather than inventing missing information.

Attachments and functioning SharePoint folder synchronization connect supporting material to individual audits. Document-informed AI assistance supports questions involving audit progress, analytical information, and policy guidance. Recorded facts, retrieved passages, and generated interpretations have different evidentiary roles. A response involving several documents does not establish exhaustive review; users must check its sources.

## Architecture and trade-offs

**Blazor Interactive Server on Azure App Service.** Application logic and data access stay on the server. Reusable Razor components let me refine the interface primarily in C#, without maintaining a separate JavaScript application. The architecture also requires interactive connections, server resources, reconnect handling, and application-state management.

**Separate responsibilities.** Interface components, feature services, business rules, and persistence have distinct responsibilities. Those boundaries add coordination work but let selected rules and failure conditions be tested independently of a full screen.

**Authorization and data integrity.** Microsoft Entra ID provides authentication. Application roles and server-side checks restrict permitted actions beyond visible controls. Managed identities support scoped backend access. Version-aware writes can reject updates based on outdated records.

## Observed result and limits

Users can investigate related records, documents, analytical information, and audit progress within a shared workspace. I have observed users updating tracker details without instruction and found the interface responsive for departmental workflows. These are qualitative observations, not formal usability or performance studies. No measured productivity, financial, or predictive-accuracy improvement is claimed; generated answers require review.

## Proposed modeling integration

The separate [machine-learning program](/work/machine-learning-audit-prioritization/) investigates predictive scores for human review prioritization. Bringing those scores into the portal is proposed integration, not an implemented decision feature. Evaluation, review, and operational approval would need to precede use in reviewer workflows.

## Operational context

The portal supports approximately 10 staff users in Audit & Oversight. I estimate that the department's broader work involves more than 5,000 attorneys: this is oversight scope, not a count of portal users or a verified current active-panel count.

CPCS [FY2027 agency appropriations](https://malegislature.gov/Budget/FY2027/FinalBudget) total approximately $380 million across agency operations, private counsel compensation, and court costs. This is agency-wide context, not spending controlled by the portal or a project outcome.

## What I learned

A small team's software can still demand careful authorization, data integrity, historical context, and maintainability. Domain feedback helps determine what an analysis or interface needs to make visible, beyond what a database alone can explain.

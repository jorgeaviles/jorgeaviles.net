## What I developed

I implemented a supervised-learning program in Python and PySpark to investigate audit prioritization. The work connects bill-level features with candidate models, repeatable evaluation, probability calibration, and scoring outputs. It extends beyond exploratory analysis into an implemented training and evaluation pipeline, while remaining experimental.

I am the sole hands-on developer of these notebooks and pipelines. Suffolk University consultation informed the early modeling proposals. Audit & Oversight colleagues contribute practical knowledge of billing and casework; their feedback helps me question features, interpret labels, and refine what an investigation needs to explain.

## Define the question before the model

The training data draws on historically randomly audited bills and recorded reduction information. The target represents a historical reduction outcome under the source's recording conventions. It is not a label for fraud, proof of improper billing, or a direct instruction to initiate a full audit.

Label construction required attention to missing information, source recording rules, and matching outcomes to the correct bill. A model learns this defined target, including the limitations of its historical coverage and review practices. A useful ranking therefore needs a narrower interpretation than “which bills are wrong?”

## Engineer features with historical context

I developed features covering billed amounts and hours, service timing, submission lag, case context, and attorney billing history. Rolling historical measurements describe prior activity, while case-level cumulative features distinguish earlier work from the current bill.

The preparation separates outcome labels from model inputs and excludes the current bill from selected historical windows. Missing grouping information needs deliberate handling so unrelated records do not acquire a shared history. These measures address leakage risks; they do not by themselves prove that every source value was available at the time a reviewer would have made a decision.

## Compare candidates on a common evaluation path

The comparison pipelines support logistic regression, Random Forest, gradient-boosted trees, and LightGBM. Model-specific preprocessing handles numerical and categorical inputs, with learned preprocessing fitted within training folds. LightGBM depends on the corresponding SynapseML runtime being available.

Random Forest became a candidate for continued experimentation. The pipeline supports selecting which candidates run and recording their evaluation metrics. I do not present a verified winning model or comparative performance figures here: those require reviewed evaluation records, rather than inferring results from a model name or notebook configuration.

## Evaluate ranking, probabilities, and time

**Grouped cross-validation.** Grouping related records by attorney or case tests a different generalization question from splitting individual rows. It helps examine whether apparent performance depends on related records appearing on both sides of a split. Grouped validation does not replace a chronological test.

**Several measures, different questions.** ROC AUC examines discrimination across thresholds. Brier score and log loss assess probability quality. Precision at a fixed review capacity asks how many historically positive outcomes appear among the highest-ranked candidates. That capacity-aware measure connects evaluation to a constrained human workflow; it is not a measure of recovered funds.

**Calibration and backtesting.** I implemented Platt calibration on a separate calibration window, evaluation on a later test window, a buffer for delayed labels, and rolling time-based backtests. These distinguish fitting a model from checking whether its scores remain useful on later data. Calibration can change probability interpretation without necessarily improving ranking.

**Drift diagnostics.** Prediction summaries and feature-distribution comparisons help investigate changes over time. Distribution drift is a signal to examine, not proof that performance has deteriorated or that a model should automatically retrain.

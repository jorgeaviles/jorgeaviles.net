## What I developed

I implemented a supervised-learning program in Python and PySpark to investigate audit prioritization. The work connects bill-level features with candidate models, repeatable evaluation, probability calibration, and scoring outputs. It extends beyond exploratory analysis into an implemented training and evaluation pipeline, while remaining experimental.

I am the sole hands-on developer of these notebooks and pipelines. Suffolk University consultation informed the early modeling proposals. Audit & Oversight colleagues contribute practical knowledge of billing and casework; their feedback helps me question features, interpret labels, and refine what an investigation needs to explain.

## Define the question before the model

The historical Random Audit program supplied reviewed bills from which to construct labeled training examples. The implemented target is whether the review's reduction field is populated: a recorded zero counts as positive, while a null or blank field counts as negative within that reviewed sample. This predicts a recording convention associated with bill review, rather than the amount reduced or selection into the random program. It is not a label for fraud, proof of improper billing, or a direct instruction to initiate a full audit.

Bills outside the matched reviewed sample do not become negative examples simply because no review outcome is available. A blank reduction field also does not independently establish that a review was complete or that billing was proper. Label construction required attention to missing information, source recording rules, and matching outcomes to the correct bill. A model learns this defined target, including the limitations of its historical coverage and review practices. A useful ranking therefore needs a narrower interpretation than “which bills are wrong?”

## Engineer features with historical context

I developed features covering billed amounts and hours, service timing, submission lag, case context, and attorney billing history. Rolling 30-, 90-, and 365-day measurements describe prior attorney activity, while case-level cumulative features distinguish earlier work from the current bill. Assignment timing, certification, and court and case attributes add context to the bill-level prediction; they are not separate case or attorney predictors.

The preparation separates outcome labels from model inputs and excludes the current bill from selected historical windows. Missing grouping information needs deliberate handling so unrelated records do not acquire a shared history. These measures address leakage risks; they do not by themselves prove that every source value was available at the time a reviewer would have made a decision.

## Evaluate ranking, probabilities, and time

**Grouped cross-validation.** The configured comparison uses ten folds grouped by BBO, the attorney identifier. Separate row- and NAC/case-grouped fold options are implemented. Holding an attorney’s related bills in one fold tests a different generalization question from splitting individual rows. It helps examine whether apparent performance depends on related records appearing on both sides of a split. Grouped validation does not replace a chronological test.

**Several measures, different questions.** ROC AUC examines discrimination across thresholds. Brier score and log loss assess probability quality. Precision at a fixed review capacity, including P@50, measures the share of historically positive labels among the top-ranked bills at that capacity. That capacity-aware measure connects evaluation to a constrained human workflow; it is not a measure of recovered funds.

**Calibration and backtesting.** I implemented Platt calibration using Spark logistic regression on the raw model probability, with a separate calibration window, a later test window, a buffer for delayed labels, and rolling time-based backtests. These routines distinguish fitting a model from checking whether its scores remain useful on later data; their presence in source does not establish completed backtests or stable performance. Calibration can change probability interpretation without necessarily improving ranking.

**Drift diagnostics.** Prediction summaries and feature-distribution comparisons help investigate changes over time. Distribution drift is a signal to examine, not proof that performance has deteriorated or that a model should automatically retrain.

## Outputs and operational boundary

The workflows implement evaluation-record and calibration registries, bill-scoring outputs, and ranked candidate views. Attorney and NAC/case history supply context for bill-level predictions; these are not independently trained attorney or case risk models. An amount-weighted ranking option multiplies a score by the bill amount; it is a prioritization heuristic, not an estimate of recoverable funds. A source review also identified a label-name mismatch between the newer feature builder and the training notebook, so the saved versions do not establish a working end-to-end run together. No numerical performance, savings, or operational impact is claimed in this public account.
